Upfront Pricing

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

No homeowner likes surprises on the final bill. So Sandy Springs HVAC quotes every Sandy Springs job before the work begins, after a complete diagnostic that looks at the equipment's age, fuel type, and tonnage, refrigerant levels, coil and heat exchanger condition, and the ductwork feeding each room. You'll know the problem, what the fix involves, and exactly what it will cost before you say yes.

A lot of HVAC failures give quiet warnings long before the system quits. Watch for rising utility bills, airflow that feels weaker than it used to, frost on the copper lines, or a bedroom that never catches up to the thermostat. Behind those symptoms are usually slow refrigerant leaks, worn blower bearings, or a capacitor on its way out. A quick inspection at the first sign often costs far less than an emergency replacement.

How old is your system? When a furnace or AC gets past the 12-year mark, it's worth thinking about the next one before the current one forces the issue. Emergency replacements happen on the worst days, in heat waves and cold snaps, when choices are limited and everyone is uncomfortable. Our team knows how the Sandy Springs climate wears on equipment and will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is running on borrowed time.

Seasonal Tune-Ups

Seasonal HVAC Maintenance Plans

Preventing a breakdown beats paying for one. That's the whole point of our maintenance plans. On each scheduled visit, we clean coils and burners, check the refrigerant charge, test the electrical components, inspect the heat exchanger, flush the condensate drain, and change the filter, all matched to the equipment in your home.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with spring cooling tune-ups and fall heating tune-ups for most systems. That's especially important in dusty areas, where dirty condenser coils choke heat rejection and drive up energy use. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your equipment's age, your household's comfort demands, and your local climate.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a regular maintenance schedule cuts operating costs. A clean coil transfers heat far more efficiently, a properly charged system stops overworking the compressor, and a fresh filter protects the blower motor. Most families find that a maintenance plan costs far less than replacing an HVAC system even one year early.

Right-Sized Systems

Equipment Sized for Your Actual House

An undersized system runs nonstop and never catches up on the hottest days, while an oversized one short-cycles, wastes energy, and leaves the house humid. Sandy Springs HVAC sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual home. That means a proper load calculation based on square footage, insulation, window exposure, and duct layout, rather than simply matching the tonnage of the old unit. For ductless mini-splits, it means matching head capacity to each zone so every room holds its setpoint.

Homes change over time. An addition or a finished basement can outgrow the single-stage system that once kept up just fine. Today's two-stage and variable-speed equipment holds tighter temperatures and uses far less energy. Whether you're comparing a heat pump and a furnace-and-AC pair, or ducted and ductless, we'll explain the real trade-offs in SEER2 ratings, operating cost, electrical and venting needs, and installation work.

Repairs We Handle

The HVAC Repairs Sandy Springs Homes Need Most

Homes in Sandy Springs run into a familiar set of heating and cooling failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Sandy Springs HVAC handles them all: no cooling, air that blows but never gets cold, furnaces that won't ignite or short-cycle, frozen evaporator coils, refrigerant leaks, condensers that hum without starting, thermostats that lose their settings, clogged condensate drains, and mini-splits flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

Not every HVAC problem is equally urgent. A weak capacitor just keeps the compressor from starting. A cracked heat exchanger, on the other hand, can let combustion gases into the house, and a backed-up condensate drain can quietly ruin a ceiling before anyone notices. Our technicians carry the parts to replace capacitors, contactors, fan motors, igniters, flame sensors, and control boards, repair leaky ducts that dump conditioned air into the attic, and recharge refrigerant once a leak has been repaired.
