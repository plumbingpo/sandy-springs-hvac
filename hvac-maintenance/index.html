Warning Signs for HVAC Maintenance

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Keep an eye out for the following clues that it's time to bring in an expert:

  • It has been more than a year since anyone opened the equipment
  • Longer run times to get to the thermostat setting
  • Recently installed equipment whose manufacturer expects maintenance records
  • Musty air when the fan starts, or a filter that has been in far too long
  • A breakdown last season that arrived with zero warning
  • Energy bills creeping up without any change in your habits
  • You simply have no idea what condition your equipment is actually in
  • A startup sound that has changed since last year

Why Hire a Professional for HVAC Maintenance?

There is a short list of things homeowners can handle: changing filters on time, keeping the outdoor coil clear, leaving registers open, and glancing at the drain. The rest takes instruments, because the problems that leave you stranded look perfectly normal. A capacitor that has lost 20 percent of its rating looks just like a new one. Low charge shows up only in superheat and subcooling readings. Cracked heat exchangers, weak flame signals, marginal pressure switches, and amp draws creeping toward the breaker's limit are all invisible until measured, and all of them cost less to fix before they fail.

With gas equipment, the stakes go beyond cost. Burners, flame proving, venting, and draft should be verified with instruments every year, because when they fail, the result can be combustion gases where they do not belong, not just a chilly house. That is why our experienced technicians treat safety testing as the center of every fall visit rather than an add-on. Over time, Sandy Springs HVAC builds a history of your readings, and the trend lines show which parts are wearing and which still have seasons left. HVAC Maintenance Prevention Tips

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Pay attention to new noises or longer cycles and mention them at your next visit
  • Test carbon monoxide detectors monthly if any of your equipment burns fuel
  • Replace your filter on a one- to three-month cycle; nothing else you do pays off more
  • Leave every supply and return register open, including those in unused rooms
  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of vegetation and rinse the coil gently each spring
  • Clear the condensate drain early in cooling season, before algae has a chance to grow
  • Schedule maintenance in early spring and early fall, ahead of the busy season and the extreme weather
